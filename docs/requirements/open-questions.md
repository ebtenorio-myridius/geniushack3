# Requirements Decisions

The original brief intentionally leaves decisions open. The following decisions make the prototype testable while preserving explicit future work.

| Question | Decision | Owner/gate | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| What is automated? | PDF text-layer extraction, schema-constrained OpenAI extraction, deterministic scoring, persistence, policy evidence retrieval, escalation flagging, telemetry, and event recording. | Engineering review | Extraction remains a draft; a failed extraction does not create a case. |
| Where does AI lead? | Runtime language-model use is structured extraction. Optional semantic policy retrieval uses embeddings; the fallback is deterministic. Evaluation cases and labels are stored fixtures, not generated by the runtime app. | FCRM review | Extraction is advisory and requires analyst review. |
| Where does deterministic logic lead? | Score combination and thresholds, workflow state transitions, vote aggregation, demo role checks on selected endpoints, and event writes. | Engineering review | Scoring and vote outcomes are reproducible, but endpoint authorization and event immutability are not consistently or database-enforced. |
| When is committee review required? | High and critical drafts are flagged; an analyst must submit a finalized case before voting can occur. The application does not force flagged cases into the queue or block their finalized status. | Analyst and governance gate | Mandatory escalation remains an implementation gap; a high-risk case can remain finalized without a committee decision. |
| What is the policy source? | Synthetic policy documents are used for the demo. Published supervisory material must be mapped and approved before production. | FCRM owner | Demo evidence is not presented as regulatory advice. |
| What happens on disagreement? | Analyst edits create a new extraction version and require a rationale. The user-facing committee route records three votes; two approvals approve, two rejections reject, and no majority defers. | Governance gate | The original structured extraction is retained, but uploaded source PDFs and full prompt/model snapshots are not. The vote rule is prototype behavior pending approval, and a lower-level direct-decision helper means the quorum is not enforced for every internal caller. |
| What data is allowed? | Synthetic data only, including generated PDFs and fictional organizations. | Demo operating rule | The app accepts PDF uploads but does not technically identify or reject real data; no real bank systems are connected. |
| What is the free-first deployment? | Docker Compose, SQLite, local storage, and optional OpenAI inference; Ollama is a future provider adapter. | Release gate | Local infrastructure is free, but OpenAI calls may cost money. |

## Current committee decision rule

The demo requires three distinct committee demo usernames. `approve` and
`approve_with_conditions` count as approvals; two approvals approve the case.
Two rejections reject it. If neither side reaches two votes, the recorded
outcome is `defer`. The rule is implemented for demonstration, not approved
institutional governance. Governance owners must decide whether a defer vote
should itself be allowed, whether a tie should remain pending, and who owns
the final decision before production use.
