### Slide 4 - Architecture: a narrow AI boundary

```text
Demo sign-in
  -> product owner uploads a text-based PDF
  -> page text + structured LLM extraction with source citations
  -> deterministic risk scoring + policy evidence lookup
  -> draft case, retained source PDF/pages, and workflow event
  -> owner submits -> analyst review -> committee decision
```

- FastAPI, Jinja2, and HTMX provide the role-specific browser workflow.
- Pydantic validates the extraction contract and cited evidence.
- SQLite stores case and workflow data; source PDFs are retained separately
  with SHA-256 integrity checks.
- Policy retrieval uses semantic matching when configured and deterministic
  rule fallback otherwise.
- PDF extraction requires the configured model provider; there is no offline
  extraction fallback.

### Slide 5 - AI harness and context boundary

- The harness is a bounded extraction step, not a multi-agent system.
- The model extracts facts from untrusted document text into a structured
  schema; source-page quotes are checked against retained PDF text.
- Prompt instructions treat embedded document directions as data, not
  authority. Prompt/model telemetry and extraction versions support review.
- Risk scoring and state transitions are deterministic code, not LLM output.
- Policy material is synthetic demonstration content, not approved guidance.

**Why:** language understanding helps structure variable submissions; scoring
and workflow transitions should be reproducible and reviewable.

### Slide 6 - Explainable scoring

| Category | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Customer and geography | 35% |
| Product and channel | 30% |
| Third party/vendor | 20% |
| Change complexity | 15% |

The scorer returns category scores and rationales, a weighted overall score,
risk level, and committee-review flag. Versioned governance rules are saved
with the case.

**Caveat:** weights, thresholds, and framework mappings are prototype rules;
control effectiveness and residual risk are not modeled.

### Slide 8 - Human-in-the-loop governance

```text
Owner draft -> Owner submits -> Analyst verifies/finalizes
  -> Analyst submits assessment -> Committee votes -> Decisioned -> Analyst closes
  -> Conditional approval: owner supplies evidence -> Analyst closes
```

- Every submitted risk level enters analyst review before committee review.
- Analysts can edit and rescore with a rationale; source citations are required
  for corrected fields.
- Information requests pause analyst or pre-vote committee review. The owner
  responds and the case resumes at its prior review stage.
- Three distinct committee identities vote. Two approvals approve, two
  rejections reject, and neither majority defers.
- An analyst may create a linked reassessment without changing the earlier
  decision; conditions require owner evidence before closure.
- Workflow events form a verifiable hash chain. A role-gated examiner export
  bundles case history, versions, votes, conditions, source material, and a
  file-hash manifest.

**Limits:** demo identities are not production authentication. Hash-chain
verification detects changes but is not database-enforced immutability; a
database owner could rewrite the chain and recompute its hashes. Vote and
scoring rules are prototype configuration, not approved institutional policy.

### Slide 9 - Working demonstration

1. Sign in as `product-owner-1` and upload
   `evals/data/sample_change_request.pdf`.
2. Inspect the draft extraction, source citations, category scores, policy
   evidence, case ID, and retained-source metadata.
3. Submit the complete draft to analyst review. If a required field is
   incomplete, correct it with source evidence before submission.
4. Switch to `analyst-1`; verify or edit/rescore with citations and rationale,
   then finalize the assessment and submit it to committee.
5. Switch among `committee-1`, `committee-2`, and `committee-3` to cast three
   distinct votes and record the committee outcome.
6. For a conditional approval, switch to the owner to submit condition
   evidence, then back to the analyst to close the case. Show examiner export
   and audit-chain verification.

An information request can be shown as an optional branch: the owner responds
and the case resumes at the analyst or committee stage where it was paused.

### Slide 11 - Failure handling and iteration

- Non-PDF, oversized, malformed, and textless uploads are rejected.
- Provider failures are explicit and do not create a draft; extraction has no
  offline fallback. Transient connection, timeout, or rate-limit failures can
  be retried up to three times.
- Source-integrity failures block source downloads and examiner exports.

### Slide 12 - Token consumption and efficiency

- Extraction uses `gpt-4o-mini` and bounds document input to 12,000 characters;
  this is a character cap, not a measured token guarantee.
- Telemetry captures model, prompt version, input/output token fields when
  returned by the provider, latency, and operation outcome.
- Next experiment: compare shorter prompt/context variants using field-level

### Slide 13 - Deployment, limitations, and next steps

**Implemented prototype:** containerized FastAPI app, SQLite case store,
role-specific demo workspaces, deterministic scoring, information requests,
three-member voting, condition tracking, reassessment, cycle-time reporting,
source-document integrity checks, and examiner-style export.

**Production gates:** real identity and hardened authorization, migrations and
backups, protected source-document storage/retention, institutionally approved
policy/scoring/decision rules, control and residual-risk modeling, externally
anchored audit retention, operational alerting, and measured token/cost
optimization.

**Closing:** prepare evidence, preserve disagreement, and keep humans
accountable.
